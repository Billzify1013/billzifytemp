How to Price Your Hotel Rooms

Most hotel rates in India were set once, years ago, based on what the property next door was charging. Here's a more useful way to think about it — grounded in what your own hotel actually does, not what someone else charges.

⚡ The Short Version

  • Your costs set your floor. They do not set your price.
  • Copying the hotel next door assumes their situation is yours. It isn't.
  • Your own booking history is the most reliable pricing guide you have.
  • A rate is a decision per date, not a decision per year.

Start With Your Floor, Not Your Price

The first number to work out isn't what to charge. It's the rate below which a booking stops being worth taking.

That figure comes from your costs — the room-level costs of housekeeping, utilities and amenities, plus a share of what the property costs to run whether occupied or not. Below it, you're paying for the privilege of having a guest.

This number is your floor. It matters enormously, and it does one job only: it tells you when to stop. It does not tell you what to charge, because what a traveller will pay has nothing to do with what your laundry costs.

Why Copying Your Neighbour Fails

The most common pricing method in Indian hospitality is looking at what the hotel down the road charges and sitting just below it.

It's understandable and it's unreliable, because their rate reflects their situation, not yours. They may have different costs, different rooms, a different debt position, a different occupancy this week. They may be making a mistake you're now copying faithfully.

There's also a compounding problem: if they're pricing off you and you're pricing off them, the whole street can drift downward together without anyone deciding to.

Your Booking History Is the Best Data You Have

You already own the most useful pricing information available, and it's usually ignored.

Look at last year. Which dates sold out, and how early? Those were underpriced. Which dates finished half empty? Those were overpriced, or needed a nudge. Which weeks were consistently strong, and which never worked no matter what?

This isn't theory — it's a record of how real travellers responded to your real rates at your actual property. No competitor's price list or industry benchmark can tell you as much.

Price the Date, Not the Room

The single biggest shift in thinking is this: a hotel room isn't one product with one price. It's a different product on every date.

A deluxe room on a wedding Saturday and the same deluxe room on a wet Tuesday are not comparable. Same furniture, same view, completely different demand — and therefore completely different value.

Once you accept that, the question changes from "what should my deluxe room cost?" to "what should this deluxe room cost on this date?" That's a harder question to answer, but it's the one that reflects reality.

The Mistakes That Cost the Most

  • Setting it once — a rate decided last year is answering last year's question.
  • Racing to the bottom — discounting to beat a neighbour, until nobody in the market is making money.
  • Refusing to move in either direction — holding firm through a sold-out festival costs as much as discounting badly.
  • Ignoring your own history — the answers are already in your data.
  • Rates that don't reach the guest — a good price sitting unpublished on one channel achieves nothing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How should I decide my hotel room rate? +
Start by working out your floor - the rate below which a booking is not worth taking, based on your costs. Then price each date according to demand, using your own booking history rather than a competitor's price list.
Should I price based on what nearby hotels charge? +
Only as background information. Their rate reflects their costs, rooms and occupancy, not yours - and they may be making a mistake you would then copy. If everyone prices off everyone else, the whole market can drift down without anyone deciding to.
What is a rate floor and why does it matter? +
It is the lowest rate at which a booking still makes sense for you, based on your costs. It tells you when to stop discounting. It does not tell you what to charge, because what a traveller will pay is a separate question.
Why is my own booking history useful for pricing? +
Because it records how real travellers responded to your real rates at your property. Dates that sold out early were underpriced; dates that finished half empty were overpriced. No external benchmark can tell you as much.
Should the same room always cost the same? +
No. The same room on a wedding Saturday and a quiet Tuesday represents completely different value to a traveller. Pricing the date rather than the room is the shift that matters most.
