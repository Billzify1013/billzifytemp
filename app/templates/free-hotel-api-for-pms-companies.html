The Problem Every PMS Provider Eventually Hits

If you build and sell hotel software in India, this conversation will be familiar. A client asks why their hotel does not show its own booking link on Google when their competitor does. You explain that it requires an integration. They ask when you will build it.

And you know what building it actually involves — delivering rates and availability to Google in the required structure, keeping the feed continuously live, handling errors, and maintaining it as things change. It is not a weekend feature. It is an ongoing engineering commitment that competes with everything else on your roadmap.

So it gets pushed back. And the request keeps coming.

Build It, Skip It, or Get It Free

There are really only three options in front of a PMS provider here.

Build it yourself. Doable, but expensive in engineering time — and the cost does not end at launch, because a feed that breaks silently is worse than no feed at all.

Skip it. Increasingly hard to justify, since it is becoming an expected feature rather than a differentiator. Clients notice its absence.

Use an API that already does it. You get the capability without the build or the maintenance, and your engineering time goes back to the parts of your product that actually differentiate you.
